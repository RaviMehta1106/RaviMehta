About Me

I’m an Electrical & Computer Engineering student at The Ohio State University with a passion for hands-on engineering, testing, and problem solving. I enjoy working on projects that combine hardware and software, analyzing data, and turning ideas into practical solutions. I’m currently building my skills in areas like circuits, embedded systems, controls, and engineering design while gaining real-world experience through engineering projects and internships.

Projects:

Handheld Taser

This project is one of my simpler ones, I had the desire to make a taser because it seemed fun and I wanted to get some practice with soldering. The materials I used consisted of a 9V battery and connector, a simple button, two nails which were cut in half, and a boost converter. Everything was wired up and soldered to make good electrical connections. The boost converter was the most important part, triggering a switch rapidly to take advantage of the properties of capacitors and inductors to exponentially increase the voltage output. Each time the current is interrupted, the inductor's collapsing magnetic field pushes energy forward through a diode into a capacitor, and the voltage there builds up higher than the input.

RC Car

This project is currently in progress. I am working with an interdisciplanary team composed of my friends (one mechanical engineering and one electrical engineering) to design and build a car that can be driven forard and backwards, turn using the rack-pinion system, and be controlled via bluetooth.

Nintendo Switch Keyboard Controller

This project is also currently in progress. This time it is a solo project. I am designing this controller to be in the format of 7 keyboard keys, each mapped to different buttons you would find on a normal nintendo switch controller. The hard part is connecting it up to the nintendo switch, and the build will involve a lot more soldering and wiring than the taser.
